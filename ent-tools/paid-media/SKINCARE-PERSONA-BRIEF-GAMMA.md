# Skincare Brand — Campaign Creative Brief
Pre-Launch Persona & Audience Analysis | 2026

Briefing a media buyer before campaign launch — understanding who we're talking to, what they fear, and what finally made them act.

- 5 Personas: Distinct buyer archetypes mapped from real customer language
- 1 Core Desire: Not clearer skin — permission to stop searching
- Clear Direction: Media strategy grounded in emotional truth and social proof

---

# Persona 1 — The Exhausted Skincare Veteran
Ages 35–55 | The chronic sufferer who has tried every trend, every product, every routine — and quietly stopped believing her skin can change.

## Who She Is
She's been through the Ordinary, the Tatcha, the prescription tretinoin, the Cerave basics, the viral TikTok actives — sometimes cycling through routines for 10, 15, 20 years. She's not uninformed. She's over-informed.

The fear isn't her skin anymore. It's the quiet resignation that this is just what her skin is now. She doesn't dare get excited. She's been burned too many times.

## Core Fear
That she'll spend money and time on another thing that doesn't work — and feel stupid for trying again.

## What Made Her Act
Specificity. When someone with her exact history — her skin type, her age, her product graveyard — said it worked. Not vague praise. Exact mirrors.

## Ad Hook Quotes
- "I've tried literally everything. The Ordinary, tretinoin, snail mucin — nothing moved the needle until this."
- "I stopped expecting results from skincare honestly. This is the first thing in years that actually changed something."
- "Was skeptical because I have combination skin, hormonal acne AND texture. This addressed all three."

## Creative Direction
Lead with product graveyard empathy. Do NOT use aspirational "glow up" language. Lead instead with specificity: real skin types, real problems named, real timelines. Social proof from women who sound exactly like her is the unlock.

---

# Persona 2 — The Confidence-Taxed Professional
Ages 28–45 | Her skin shows up in her work life — on Zoom calls, in-person presentations, first meetings. The condition isn't just physical. It's professional.

## Who She Is
She checks her skin before important calls. She's adjusted her camera angle. She's worn more makeup than she wants to just to feel okay in meetings. She's high-performing. She's used to solving problems. Her skin is the one thing she can't fix — and that bothers her disproportionately because she fixes everything else.

## Core Fear
Being perceived as someone who doesn't take care of herself. The gap between how competent she is and how her skin makes her feel.

## What Made Her Act
The promise of being able to stop managing the problem and actually solving it. Efficiency matters: results without a 12-step routine.

## Ad Hook Quotes
- "I started a new job and my skin was just... a lot. This cleared everything in six weeks. I finally feel like myself again at work."
- "I'm on Zoom all day. I literally rescheduled meetings because of a flare. Now I don't think about it anymore."
- "I needed something that actually worked without taking 45 minutes every night. This is it."

## Creative Direction
Lead with the before context — the specific professional situation where skin was costing her something. Efficiency is a real hook: "works fast," "simple routine," "real results." Before/after in professional contexts will resonate more than glamour shots.

# Persona 3 — The Research-First Skeptic
Ages 30–50 | The buyer who has done the math on what they've wasted. They need proof before they'll spend another dollar.

## Who He Is
Found the brand through Reddit, a derm recommendation, or a comparison review site — not an ad. Has built a mental spreadsheet of ingredients tried. Knows the difference between niacinamide and tranexamic acid. He's rationally locked out, not emotionally. He's spent hundreds on underdelivering products and needs a mechanism, not a feeling.

## Core Fear
Being fooled by marketing again. Buying something that sounds good but has no real science behind it.

## What Made Him Act
Third-party validation from someone who researches like he does. Ingredient transparency. A specific claim with a specific reason why.

## Ad Hook Quotes
- "Found this on a Reddit thread comparing barrier repair serums. Read the ingredient list and was impressed. It actually works."
- "I'm an ingredient nerd so I was already familiar with the actives. The formulation is clean. My skin barrier hasn't been this good in years."
- "I don't trust ads. I trust reviews from people who clearly know what they're talking about. Multiple sources pointed here."

## Creative Direction
Lead with ingredient logic, mechanism, or third-party validation. Reddit threads, derm recommendations, ingredient breakdowns. Avoid aspirational language — this buyer wants to understand WHY it works. Avoid: "glowing skin," "confidence," "transform." Do: "here's why this works," "here's what's in it," "here's what the research says."

# Persona 4 — The Value-Conscious Loyalist
Ages 25–42 | The converted believer held back by price — buying for herself and often her household. This is a necessity, not a luxury.

## Who She Is
She already believes the product works. That's not the issue. She's done the research, seen the results on people like her. But the price point makes her hesitate every time she goes to add to cart. She might be buying for a teenager with acne, for her own recurring issue, for her partner — this is ongoing, not one-time. She's calculating cost-per-use and asking whether she can justify this long-term.

## Core Fear
Investing in something that works but that she can't realistically sustain — and then going backward.

## What Made Her Act
Framing that made the math work. A bundle, a subscription discount, a "costs less than one dermatologist co-pay" comparison. The reframe from luxury to investment.

## Ad Hook Quotes
- "Finally found something that works and then saw the price. But once I did the math — cheaper than prescription visits every quarter."
- "I was on the fence because of cost. The bundle option made it possible. Worth every penny."
- "I buy this for my daughter and myself now. Expensive but we stopped buying the 4 other things that didn't work."

## Creative Direction
Bundle offers, family/household framing, subscription savings, cost-per-use breakdown, and comparison to the cumulative cost of failing alternatives. Frame as medical necessity, not luxury skincare. Media buyer note: test bundle CTA vs. single unit CTA — this persona converts significantly better on value packages.

# Persona 5 — The Newly Triggered
Ages 22–38 | Developed a new skin issue from a hormonal change, stress event, environmental shift, or medication — still in the acute phase, desperate for a fast answer.

## Who She Is
This is new for her. She didn't grow up with this. Something changed — a birth control switch, a move, a stressful year, a medication side effect, pregnancy, perimenopause — and suddenly her skin doesn't feel like hers. She's in the Google spiral. She's tried three things in two weeks. She hasn't given up yet — she's in the early urgency phase.

## Core Fear
That this is permanent. That she missed the window to fix it. That her skin is different now and there's no going back.

## What Made Her Act
A clear timeline to results — days, not months. Someone who went through exactly what triggered her condition and came out the other side quickly.

## Ad Hook Quotes
- "My skin completely changed after going off the pill. Nothing worked until this — saw a difference in less than two weeks."
- "Moved to a new state and my skin freaked out. This calmed everything down within 10 days."
- "Started breaking out for the first time at 34. I was panicking. This is the only thing that actually helped and fast."

## Creative Direction
Lead with speed-to-results. Specific timelines ("first 5 days," "within two weeks," "noticeable by day 7") are the most important creative element. Trigger identification in hooks ("if your skin changed after...") will stop the scroll. Reassurance over aspiration. Do NOT position as luxury skincare — position as a solution to a specific, recent, reversible problem.

# Media Buyer Summary

## The 1 Core Desire Across All 5 Personas
Not clearer skin — permission to stop searching.

Every persona has been let down before. The emotional job of the creative is not to sell features. It's to make the right buyer feel: "This is the one."

## Hook Formula by Persona
- Exhausted Veteran → Product graveyard empathy + specificity. Avoid glow language.
- Confidence-Taxed Professional → The professional cost of bad skin. Avoid clinical language.
- Research-First Skeptic → Ingredient logic + third-party validation. Avoid vague claims.
- Value-Conscious Loyalist → Cost-per-use math + bundle framing. Avoid luxury positioning.
- Newly Triggered → Speed-to-results + trigger identification. Avoid long-term promises.

## Universal Creative Rules
- Use real customer language, not marketing language — the best hooks live in reviews
- Specificity converts — "if you've tried tretinoin, ACV, and steroid creams..." beats "it works!"
- Social proof is the unlock for skeptical, tried-everything buyers
- Ad comments reveal your best objections and your best hooks — mine them before writing
- Fix in this order: hook (thumbstop) → body (hold rate) → offer (CPA)

# Dara Denney's Creative Strategy Research SOP
The 5-step process that powers winning creatives — manual first, AI second.

- Step 1: Reputation Analysis
- Step 2: Customer Review Mining
- Step 3: Persona Segmentation
- Step 4: Past Performance Analysis
- Step 5: Competitor Research

## The 6 Context Documents You'll Build
- 01 — Reputation Analysis Document
- 02 — Customer Review Mining Sheet
- 03 — Triggers, Objections, Transformations Document
- 04 — Audience Segmentation (Personas) + Signals
- 05 — Past Performance Document
- 06 — Competitors Document

---

# Step 1 — Reputation Analysis
Mimics the exact research a customer does when deciding if your product is right for them. Always the first step.

## The 3-Phase Workflow
- Duplicate: Copy the Reputation Analysis template for the brand
- Fill Completely: Crucial for getting great AI results later — this is your first context document
- Ideate: By the end, you should already have creative ideas forming

## Where to Research
- Reddit threads about the brand and category
- Google and Trustpilot reviews
- YouTube comments on brand and competitor videos
- Facebook group discussions
- Ad comments on existing ads

---

# Step 2 — Customer Review Mining
Systematic extraction of buyer psychology from real customer language.

## What to Pull
- CSV of all customer reviews (export from platform)
- Ad comments — these are critical. They reveal objections in real time.
- Website and Amazon reviews per SKU (rank by # of reviews and rating)
- Trustpilot reviews
- Minimum 50 reviews per product

## AI Prompts to Run on Your Reviews
- Write a SWOT analysis for [BRAND] based on these reviews
- Summarize the overall sentiment — what emotions come up most?
- What are the 10 most common complaints or objections?
- Pull 20 "golden nugget phrases" that could be used verbatim as Meta ad hooks
- What trigger points caused someone to finally buy?
- What were the biggest objections customers had BEFORE buying?
- Identify 5 distinct buyer types from these reviews
- Write 10 first-person ad hook openers that sound like a real customer

---

# Step 3 — Persona Segmentation
The most important step. This research illuminates who you actually need to be targeting with your creatives.

## The Process
- Step 1: Feed your Reputation Analysis + Review Mining outputs to AI and ask it to identify distinct buyer personas
- Step 2: Pick the 3–5 audience segments with the most evidence and real potential

## Each Persona Card Must Include
- Archetype name + representative real names
- Age range and demographic
- Psychographic one-liner (their mindset in a sentence)
- Who they are: history with the problem
- Core fear: the emotion underneath the symptom
- What made them act: the specific trigger that converts
- 3 real ad hook quotes pulled from review mining
- Creative direction: specific brief for the media buyer

---

# Step 4 — Past Performance Analysis
What worked before, why it worked, and who it was working for.

## What to Analyze
- Which creatives generated the most purchases at the lowest CPA
- Thumbstop rate (strong benchmark: 50%+)
- Hold rate (strong benchmark: 18%+)
- Which persona each winning creative was speaking to
- Hook types correlated with best performance

## Key Metrics
- Thumbstop Rate → Did the hook stop the scroll?
- Hold Rate → Did the body hold attention?
- CPA → What did a purchase cost?
- Purchase Volume → Scale and proof of concept

---

# Step 5 — Competitor Research
Find the gaps — what your audience wants to hear that no one is saying.

## What to Document
- Top competitor brands in the category (Meta Ad Library audit)
- Their most-run ads and what hooks they lead with
- What angles are oversaturated — and what's missing
- Price positioning and how competitors frame value
- Comment sections on competitor ads (reveals unmet needs)

---
