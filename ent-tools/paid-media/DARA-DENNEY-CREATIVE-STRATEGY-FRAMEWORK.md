## THE CORE PHILOSOPHY

> "The thing that is really powering creatives in 2026 is to make sure that you're actually targeting the right audiences. Which means you really need to know your personas or your audience segments like the back of your hand."

> "I often find that 'AI RESEARCH' doesn't quite stick as well as doing it the old fashioned way — manually. Always do manual research as a first pass, then upload your context documents to AI."

**KEY PRINCIPLE: Always do manual research first. Never skip this.**

---

## THE 5-STEP CREATIVE STRATEGY RESEARCH PROCESS

### Outputs: 6 Context Documents
By the end of the full research process, you produce these foundational documents that power all creative production:

| # | Document |
|---|----------|
| 01 | Reputation Analysis Document |
| 02 | Customer Review Mining Sheet |
| 03 | Triggers, Objections, Transformations Document |
| 04 | Audience Segmentation (Personas) + Signals |
| 05 | Past Performance Document |
| 06 | Competitors Document |

---

## STEP 1: REPUTATION ANALYSIS

**What it is:** Mimics the exact research a customer does when deciding if your product is right for them. Always the first step during brand onboarding.

**Three-Phase Workflow:**
1. **Duplicate** — Copy the Reputation Analysis template for the brand
2. **Fill Completely** — This is crucial for getting great AI results later. It's your first context document.
3. **Ideate** — By the end, you should already have creative ideas forming

**What to research:**
- Reddit threads about the brand and category
- Google reviews
- App store reviews (if applicable)
- YouTube comments on competitor/brand videos
- Trustpilot
- Facebook group discussions
- DMs / support tickets (if brand has access)

**Output:** Reputation Analysis Document — a comprehensive picture of how the brand is perceived, what language customers use, what fears exist, and what credibility signals matter.

---

## STEP 2: CUSTOMER REVIEW MINING

**What it is:** Systematic extraction of the most important buyer psychology from real customer language.

### What to Pull:
- CSV of all customer reviews (export from platform)
- Ad comments — *these are critical. They reveal objections in real time.*
- Website reviews per SKU
- Amazon reviews (rank by # of reviews and rating)
- Trustpilot reviews
- Minimum: **50 reviews per product**

### AI Prompts to Run on Collected Reviews:
1. Write a SWOT analysis for [BRAND]
2. Summarize comments and overall sentiment
3. What are the most common complaints about [product]?
4. What are the "golden nugget phrases" for Meta Ads from these reviews?
5. What trigger points caused someone to buy?
6. What were the biggest objections before buying?

**Prioritize:** Products by number of reviews or sales. Compile AI responses and use human judgment to highlight the most important insights.

---

## STEP 3: PERSONA SEGMENTATION (The Most Important Step)

> "This is where the magic happens. The research process is going to illuminate who you actually need to be targeting with your creatives."

**Two-Phase Process:**
1. **Use AI to determine customer personas** — feed it your Reputation Analysis + Review Mining outputs
2. **Pick the audience segments with the most evidence and potential** — choose 3-5 that are clearly distinct and have real data behind them

### Persona Card Framework (Per Persona):
Each persona card includes:
- **Name:** Archetype title + representative real first names
- **Demographics:** Age range
- **Psychographic tagline:** One-line mindset description
- **Who They Are:** 2-3 sentence profile including history with the problem
- **Core Fear:** The emotional driver underneath the symptom
- **What Made Them Act:** The specific trigger or content type that converts them
- **Ad Hook Quotes:** 3 real customer quotes that sound like this person talking (pulled from review mining)
- **Creative Direction:** Specific instruction for the media buyer on messaging angle and what to avoid

---

## STEP 4: PAST PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS

**What to analyze:**
- Which creatives generated the most purchases at lowest CPA
- Thumbstop rate (benchmark: strong = 50%+)
- Hold rate (benchmark: strong = 18%+)
- Which persona segment each winning creative was speaking to
- Hook types that correlated with performance

**Key Metrics to Track:**

| Metric | What it Tells You |
|--------|-------------------|
| Thumbstop Rate | Did the hook stop the scroll? |
| Hold Rate | Did the body hold attention? |
| CPA | What did a purchase cost? |
| Purchase Volume | Scale of proof |

**Real Example from Dara's Data:**
- Spend: $42,566 | Purchases: 857 | CPA: $49.67
- Thumbstop: **59.48%** | Hold Rate: **19.64%**
- Pattern: Social proof from someone who "sounded exactly like them" was the hook formula

---

## STEP 5: COMPETITOR RESEARCH & MARKET ANALYSIS

**What to document:**
- Top competitor brands in the category
- Their most-run ads (use Meta Ad Library)
- What hooks competitors are leading with
- Gaps — what's NOT being said that your audience actually wants to hear
- Price positioning and how competitors frame value

# SKINCARE BRAND — CAMPAIGN CREATIVE BRIEF
## Pre-Launch Persona & Audience Analysis | 2026

**Briefing:** Understanding who we're talking to, what they fear, and what finally made them act — before a single creative is produced.

**Summary:**

| 5 Personas | 1 Core Desire | Clear Direction |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Distinct buyer archetypes mapped from real customer language | Not clearer skin — *permission to stop settling* | Media strategy grounded in emotional truth, social proof, and buyer-specific triggers |

---

## PERSONA 1: The Exhausted Skincare Veteran

**Ages 35–55** | *The chronic sufferer who has tried every trend, every product, every routine — and quietly stopped believing her skin can change.*

### Who She Is
She's been through the Ordinary, the Tatcha, the prescription tretinoin, the Cerave basics, the viral TikTok actives — sometimes cycling through routines for 10, 15, 20 years. She's not uninformed. She's over-informed. She knows the ingredients, reads the studies, has an entire shelf of "almost worked" products.

The fear isn't her skin anymore. It's the quiet resignation that **this is just what her skin is now.** She doesn't dare get excited. She's been burned too many times.

### Core Fear
That she'll spend money and time on another thing that doesn't work — and feel stupid for trying again.

### What Made Her Act
Specificity. When someone with her exact history — her skin type, her age, her product graveyard — said it worked. Not vague praise. Exact mirrors.

### Ad Hook Quotes (write ads that sound like these):
> "I've tried literally everything. The Ordinary, tretinoin, snail mucin — nothing moved the needle until this."

> "I stopped expecting results from skincare honestly. This is the first thing in years that actually changed something."

> "Was skeptical because I have combination skin, hormonal acne AND texture. This addressed all three."

### Creative Direction
Lead with product graveyard empathy. Do NOT use aspirational "glow up" language — she's heard it. Lead instead with specificity: real skin types, real problems named, real timelines. Social proof from women who sound exactly like her is the unlock. The creative should feel like she found her people, not a sales pitch.

---

## PERSONA 2: The Confidence-Taxed Professional

**Ages 28–45** | *Her skin shows up in her work life — on Zoom calls, in-person presentations, first meetings. The condition isn't just physical. It's professional.*

### Who She Is
She checks her skin before important calls. She's adjusted her camera angle. She's worn more makeup than she wants to just to feel okay in meetings. The condition — whether it's redness, acne, hyperpigmentation, or texture — is invisible to everyone else but dominates her mental real estate.

She's high-performing. She's used to solving problems. Her skin is the one thing she can't seem to fix, and that bothers her disproportionately because **she fixes everything else.**

### Core Fear
Being perceived as someone who doesn't take care of herself. The gap between how competent she is and how her skin makes her feel.

### What Made Her Act
The promise of being able to stop *managing* the problem and actually *solving* it. Efficiency matters: results without a 12-step routine.

### Ad Hook Quotes (write ads that sound like these):
> "I started a new job and my skin was just... a lot. This cleared my back and face in six weeks. I finally feel like myself again at work."

> "I'm on Zoom all day. I literally rescheduled meetings because of a flare. Now I don't think about it anymore."

> "I needed something that actually worked without taking 45 minutes every night. This is it."

### Creative Direction
Lead with the before context — the specific professional situation where skin was costing her something. Avoid clinical or medicalized language; she wants to solve a confidence problem, not treat a condition. Efficiency is a real hook: "works fast," "simple routine," "real results" land better than ingredient-first messaging. Before/after in professional contexts (not just glamour shots) will resonate.

---

## PERSONA 3: The Research-First Skeptic

**Ages 30–50** | *The buyer who has done the math on what they've wasted. They need proof before they'll spend another dollar.*

### Who He Is
Found the brand through Reddit, a dermatologist recommendation, or a comparison review site — not an ad. Has already built a spreadsheet of ingredients they've tried. Knows the difference between niacinamide and tranexamic acid. Checks Deciem ingredient lists before buying.

He's not emotionally locked out of the category — he's rationally locked out. He's spent hundreds on products that underdelivered, and **he needs a mechanism, not a feeling.**

### Core Fear
Being fooled by marketing again. Buying something that sounds good but has no real science behind it.

### What Made Him Act
Third-party validation from someone who researches like he does. Ingredient transparency. A specific claim with a specific reason why.

### Ad Hook Quotes (write ads that sound like these):
> "Found this on a Reddit thread comparing barrier repair serums. Read the ingredient list and was impressed. It actually works."

> "I'm an ingredient nerd so I was already familiar with the actives. The formulation is clean. My skin barrier hasn't been this good in years."

> "I don't trust ads. I trust reviews from people who clearly know what they're talking about. Multiple sources pointed here."

### Creative Direction
Lead with ingredient logic, mechanism, or third-party validation. Reddit threads, derm recommendations, ingredient breakdowns. Avoid aspirational or emotional language — this buyer wants to understand *why* it works. Testimonials that reference specific ingredients or comparisons to other products he's tried will land hardest. Avoid: "glowing skin," "confidence," "transform." Do: "here's why this works," "here's what's different," "here's what the research says."

---

## PERSONA 4: The Value-Conscious Loyalist

**Ages 25–42** | *The converted believer held back by price — buying for herself and often her household. This is a necessity, not a luxury.*

### Who She Is
She already believes the product works. That's not the issue. She's done the research, seen the results on people like her, maybe even tried a sample. But the price point makes her hesitate every time she goes to add to cart.

She might be buying for a teenager with acne, for her own recurring issue, for her partner — this is ongoing, not one-time. She's calculating cost-per-use and asking herself whether she can justify this long-term.

### Core Fear
Investing in something that works but that she can't realistically sustain. And then going backward.

### What Made Her Act
Framing that made the math work. A bundle, a subscription discount, a "costs less than one dermatologist co-pay" comparison. The reframe from luxury to investment.

### Ad Hook Quotes (write ads that sound like these):
> "Finally found something that works and then saw the price. But once I did the math — cheaper than prescription visits every quarter."

> "I was on the fence because of cost. The bundle option made it possible. Worth every penny."

> "I buy this for my daughter and myself now. Expensive but we stopped buying the other 4 things that didn't work."

### Creative Direction
Bundle offers, family/household framing, subscription savings, cost-per-use breakdown, and comparison to the cumulative cost of failing alternatives. Frame as medical necessity, not luxury skincare. Do not lead with aspirational imagery — lead with practical math and emotional relief that the search is over. Media buyer note: test bundle CTA vs. single unit CTA; this persona converts significantly better on value packages.

---

## PERSONA 5: The Newly Triggered

**Ages 22–38** | *Developed a new skin issue from a hormonal change, stress event, environmental shift, or medication — still in the acute phase, desperate for a quick answer.*

### Who She Is
This is new for her. She didn't grow up with acne. She didn't have redness before. Something changed — a birth control switch, a move to a new city, a stressful year, a medication side effect, pregnancy, perimenopause — and suddenly her skin doesn't feel like hers.

She's in the Google spiral. She's tried three things in two weeks. She's anxious, not resigned. She hasn't given up yet — she's in the early urgency phase.

### Core Fear
That this is permanent. That she missed the window to fix it. That her skin is different now and there's no going back.

### What Made Her Act
A clear timeline to results — days, not months. Someone who went through exactly what triggered her condition and came out the other side quickly. Reassurance that this is reversible.

### Ad Hook Quotes (write ads that sound like these):
> "My skin completely changed after going off the pill. Nothing worked until this — saw a difference in less than two weeks."

> "Moved to a new state and my skin freaked out. This calmed everything down within 10 days."

> "Started breaking out for the first time at 34. I was panicking. This is the only thing that actually helped and fast."

### Creative Direction
Lead with speed-to-results. Specific timelines ("first 5 days," "within two weeks," "noticeable by day 7") are the most important creative element for this persona. Trigger identification in hooks ("if your skin changed after [event]...") will stop the scroll. Reassurance language over aspirational language — "this is fixable" over "get glowing skin." Urgency-driven CTAs work. Do NOT position this as a luxury skincare product to this buyer — position it as a solution to a specific, recent, reversible problem.

## MEDIA BUYER SUMMARY

**The 1 Core Desire Across All 5 Personas:**
Not clearer skin. **Permission to stop searching.**

Every persona has been let down before — by products, by promises, by routines. The emotional job of the creative is not to sell features. It's to make the right buyer feel: *"This is the one."*

**Hook Formula by Persona:**

| Persona | Lead With | Avoid |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| Exhausted Veteran | Product graveyard empathy + specificity | Aspirational glow language |
| Confidence-Taxed Professional | The professional cost of bad skin | Clinical/condition language |
| Research-First Skeptic | Ingredient logic + third-party validation | Emotional, vague claims |
| Value-Conscious Loyalist | Cost-per-use math + bundle framing | Luxury positioning |
| Newly Triggered | Speed-to-results + trigger identification | Long-term promises |

**Universal Creative Principles (Dara's Rules):**
1. Use real customer language, not marketing language — the best hooks are found in reviews, not copywriters' heads
2. Specificity converts — vague claims ("it works!") lose to mirrored specificity ("if you've tried X, Y, Z...")
3. Social proof is the unlock for skeptical, tried-everything buyers — testimonials should sound like the persona, not a generic success story
4. Ad comments reveal your best objections and your best hooks — mine them before writing a single script
5. Thumbstop rate tells you if the hook is working. Hold rate tells you if the body is working. CPA tells you if the offer is working. Fix in that order.

## REVIEW MINING QUICK-START: AI PROMPT STACK

Run these prompts on your collected review CSV + ad comments:

```
1. "Write a SWOT analysis for [BRAND] based on these customer reviews."

2. "Summarize the overall sentiment of these reviews. What emotions come up most?"

3. "What are the 10 most common complaints or objections in these reviews?"

4. "Pull 20 'golden nugget' phrases from these reviews that could be used verbatim as Meta ad hooks or UGC scripts."

5. "What trigger points caused someone to finally buy this product? List the top 10."

6. "What were the biggest objections customers had BEFORE buying? How did they overcome them?"

7. "Identify 5 distinct buyer types from these reviews. For each, describe their situation before buying, their main fear, and what finally convinced them."

8. "Write 10 ad hook openers in first-person voice that sound like a real customer, based on these reviews."
```

## DARA'S CREATIVE RESEARCH CHECKLIST

**Before producing a single creative:**

- [ ] Reputation Analysis Document complete (Reddit, Google, App Store, YouTube comments)
- [ ] Review Mining Sheet complete (minimum 50 reviews per product, ad comments exported)
- [ ] AI prompts run on review data, human judgment applied to highlight best insights
- [ ] Triggers, Objections, Transformations Document built
- [ ] 3-5 Personas defined with real evidence from reviews
- [ ] Past Performance Analysis done (if existing account) — top creatives mapped to persona
- [ ] Competitors Document built (Meta Ad Library audit, hook analysis)
- [ ] Context documents uploaded to AI before prompting for creative concepts
- [ ] Media buyer briefed on persona signals and creative direction per segment

**Remember:**
> "Don't skip this. By the end of this research process, you will have enough context to make your AI results dramatically better — and your creative team will finally know exactly who they're making content for."
